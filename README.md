# LocalFood-Connect-AI-for-local-food-systems
Building AI course project

# Connecting local production with local demand

## Summary:
LocalFood Connect is a proposed AI-enabled platform that connects small-scale food producers with nearby consumers, restaurants, retailers, producer markets and institutions. It would combine available supply, buyer demand, product perishability, location and timing to recommend potential transactions.
Its objectives are to improve market access for small producers, strengthen short food supply chains and reduce avoidable food losses. AI would support—not replace—commercial decisions and local relationships.

## Project
Background:
Small-scale producers may struggle to reach buyers quickly, while local buyers may lack up-to-date information about seasonal availability. This information gap is particularly costly for fresh, perishable produce.
FAO and UNEP identify food loss and waste as major sustainability challenges. Short food supply chains can improve direct connections, although their environmental benefits depend on transport, logistics and other local conditions.
The project is especially relevant to SDG 12, Target 12.3 on food loss and waste, and also relates to SDGs 2, 8, 11 and 13.

Data and AI techniques:
Producer inputs could include product type, available quantity, asking price, location, harvest date and expected shelf life. Buyer inputs could include requested quantity, budget, delivery window, location and purchasing preferences. Historical transactions and seasonality could inform demand estimates.
Machine-learning forecasting could estimate near-term demand where sufficient reliable data exist. A recommendation or ranking system could suggest buyer–producer matches using product compatibility, quantity, timing, price, distance and perishability. Simple rules would provide a useful baseline before introducing more complex models.
Any forecast or ranking should show the underlying factors and uncertainty. Current inventory must be confirmed by participants; predicted availability is not a guarantee.

How is it used?:
A producer lists 120 kg of tomatoes available Tuesday through Thursday. Local restaurants, retailers and market vendors register their requirements. The system identifies compatible buyers and ranks potential matches based on quantity, distance, price and timing.
For example, a nearby restaurant seeking 20 kg this week may receive a recommendation. The producer chooses whether to contact the buyer; the buyer confirms the order. No transaction is automatic.
Workflow: Producer supply + Buyer needs → Data validation → AI forecast and matching → Suggested connections → Producer/buyer confirmation → Local sale → Feedback and impact monitoring.
Users could include farmers, cooperatives, markets, restaurants, hotels, institutional buyers, consumers and local governments.

Challenges:
Reliable data are difficult to maintain when inventories and prices change quickly. Small producers may face connectivity, cost or digital-literacy barriers; the interface should be mobile-friendly and allow cooperative-assisted entry.
A digital match does not solve transport, refrigeration, payment or quality-assurance problems. The platform should avoid favoring large buyers unfairly or exposing commercially sensitive data.
Forecasts may fail during extreme weather or unexpected demand shifts. Food-loss reductions and emissions savings must be measured, not assumed.

What next?:
Pilot the concept in one region with a small number of producers and buyers, focusing on perishable fruits and vegetables. Start with straightforward matching and add demand forecasting only when sufficient transaction history becomes available.
Track successful matches, sales volume, time to sale, unsold produce, user satisfaction and participation by smaller producers. Compare results with a pre-pilot baseline to test whether the platform actually reduces losses.
If the pilot works, explore transport coordination, cooperative participation, surplus donation channels and integration with local market information services.
